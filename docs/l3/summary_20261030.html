Layer 3 gate: overnight findings

Falcon social navigation, shared Layer 3 Query Selector · night of 29–30 October 2026 · paired dev evaluations against the default-policy floor

Bottom line

No legal selector beats the floor. The 85% gate (best configuration, legal 90° camera) is out of reach in the 2–3 day window: my estimate is under 5%.

The night measured why. An exact evaluator that simulates each candidate for real finds the fixes, and its choices hold up under timing noise. What is missing is an evaluator that can reproduce the robot's own closed loop: what its camera will see, which map it will build, how its planner reacts. Learned critics cannot do this, and RL on them made the policy worse. It was stopped at 04:57.

What the night established

  • The query set is good enough. Exact one-step evaluation solves 21 of 24 episodes vs the floor's 14. Adding scan queries changes nothing (also 21).
  • Its wins are not luck. Shifting every person by ±0.1 s leaves 86% of candidate outcomes unchanged. 10 of 12 critical states have a fix that survives the shifts. The timing-robust reference keeps 20 of the exact reference's 21 successes.
  • Learned critics cannot score candidates. At critical states they pick no better than random, even when given the people's true future. The latest RL checkpoint lost to the floor 5 to 18 (p = 0.011).
  • The gap is the fidelity of the robot's own closed loop. With the true future of every person, the release imagination still drifts 1.25 m from reality within 3 s. Simulating the camera and progressive map discovery cuts that to 0.49 m.
  • The camera model works only with perfect people prediction. With an outcome-ordered rule it reaches 63.9% on ORCA and 61.1% on SFM (72 episodes each, p = 0.004 and 0.031); DWA gains little (54.2% vs 48.6%). Pooled over the three planners: 41 wins / 15 losses, p = 0.0007. The same selector with Stage 1 worlds stays at the floor under either rule.
  • It needs everyone's future over the whole window, and accurately. A perfect 3 s prediction, or the long future of the tracked people only, recovers less than half of the gain. A velocity error of just 0.1 m/s per person loses more than half of it too, even with every unseen person roughly placed.

What I believe now

  • 85% under the legal benchmark within the 2–3 day window: under 5%.
  • This is not an information wall: the timing-robust exact evaluator still finds the fixes. It is a simulation wall. The selector must re-run the robot's camera, map and planner inside a predicted world, with people futures over 10 s or more, including people not yet seen.
  • A legal selector at 60–65% on ORCA-native within weeks: about 30–40%. 85% eventually: about 10%.

Decisions for you

  1. The gate. Keep 85% for the legal benchmark, or re-baseline the near-term target to beating the published state of the art (about 60%)?
  2. Layer 3 direction. I recommend re-scoping around closed-loop imagination, with learning on top of imagined outcomes. The camera model is done and legal Stage 2 map discovery is built. RL on compiled features is finished.
  3. Stage 1. The decisive dependency is a 10–15 s, multi-world people horizon that includes people not yet seen. This needs the Stage 1 owner.
  4. The executor. The legal selector's collision savings turn into timeouts. 41% of floor collisions happen while the robot turns in place, and most deadlocks have no 1–2-step escape. These are executor-level problems.
